# Investment case: whether to adopt a sector rotation sleeve

A US equity manager primarily obtains market exposure through SPY. The team proposes allocating 20% of the account to sector rotation, holding the three sectors with the strongest medium-term performance each month. The manager must judge whether sector selection, concentration and trading add enough net value to justify the extra burden.

Historical market data are real; the firm, account, allocation and costs are hypothetical. A $100 million account illustrates dollar effects without establishing institutional trading capacity.

## From proposal to executable review

The researcher defines the proposal: nine original sector ETFs, MOM12-1 ranking, equal weights in the top three, month-end decisions and execution at the next session's close. Existing positions earn execution-day returns. Positions drift before trading, and fees are deducted from portfolio wealth.

Three questions determine the comparisons:

| Question | Comparison and constraint |
|---|---|
| What does sector selection add to simple diversification? | Monthly equal-weighting in the same universe, with random three-sector diagnostics |
| Is the signal still worthwhile after trading? | Actual position drift, fees on purchases and sales, and fixed 0/5/10 bps scenarios |
| What happens to the account with a 20% sleeve? | SPY core and blended accounts, evaluated on active return, tracking error and adverse paths |

Price changes between the decision and the following close belong to the old portfolio. Dividends are not added again to adjusted-price returns. Unchanged membership can still require trading to restore equal weights. These constraints convert a paper ranking into reconcilable holdings and cash records. See the [research design](research_design.md).

## Evidence and the manager's decision

The review considers full-period returns, risk, costs and uncertainty together. History includes profits with outperformance, profits with underperformance, losses smaller than the benchmark's, and losses with underperformance. Event windows explain how continuous holdings behaved.

Follow-up research reconciles selected-versus-omitted sector P&L and examines limited conditions known before trading, actual filters and external industry portfolios. Concentrated controls place the strategy's historical return near the middle of random distributions, while its maximum drawdown is shallower. These observations do not establish a reliable enablement rule or stable net incremental value. Numerical results and evidence boundaries are consolidated in the [final review](final_review.md).

The manager therefore **does not add the momentum sleeve and retains the existing SPY policy allocation**. Meeting an assumed risk budget does not supply a reason to spend it. Sector equal-weighting's historical lead also does not approve a new allocation. See the [manager memo](final_manager_memo.md).

## Scope at closure

Phase one ends with a reproducible decision. Frozen rules and genuinely new decision and implementation records would be necessary for prospective evidence. Explaining why sector advantages persist or reverse would require point-in-time earnings expectations, valuations or policy information and a separate protocol.
